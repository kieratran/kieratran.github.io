My Projects

Radar-derived basal melt rates of Ross Ice Shelf, Antractica

This project examines the relationship between airborne ice-penetrating radar attenuation rates and ice-shelf basal melt rates through ice temperature at Ross Ice Shelf (RIS). We build a 1.5-D thermal model to capture this direct relationship and convert melt rates from depth-resolved multiple-reflector attenuation rates. Radar data was from ROSETTA-Ice airborne survey. We discover higher basal melt rates at grounding zones of both eastern and western RIS, which reveal complex mixtures of melting processes caused by tides, ocean currents, subglacial hydrology discharge, and basal topography. This work is currently in prep.

Evolution of ice-shelf basal channels at Pine Island shear margin

This is a NASA FINESST project with collaboration with Dr. Karen Alley and her students (Adam Isaak & Dylan Kreynen), who develop an automated algorithm to detect surface depression of ice-shelf basal channels. Simultaneously, I run swath analysis to obtain 3-D images of basal topography to observe crevasse formation at the channel apex. Together, we investigate the evolution of basal channels that links with ice-shelf collapse at PIG.

Understanding hydrostatic equilibrium through physics-informed machine learning model

I am also interested in understanding hydrostatic imbalance near the grounding zone, where the assumptions of hydrostatic equilibrium become unreliable. I plan to develop a physics-informed machine learning model trained on the relationship between surface and basal elevation observed with ice-penetrating radar. This model could be used to infer plausible basal elevations across Antarctic ice shelves, particularly in regions where direct observations are limited. Ultimately, this approach could help us better understand basal geometry and the transition from grounded to floating ice.
